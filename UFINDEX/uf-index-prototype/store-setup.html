00

Two things this choice brings with it

Neither blocks enrolment. Both are cheaper to handle now than after the accounts are live.

  • Ask about the Apple fee waiver first. Apple waives the $99 annual fee for some eligible non-profits, and a foundation is exactly the kind of entity it exists for. Worth checking before paying — it is a recurring cost, not a one-off, and the check takes minutes.
  • Get the subscription revenue confirmed by whoever does UFAS's accounts. UF Index charges ₹399/month, and commercial income arriving through a foundation is a tax and compliance question rather than an app question. The stores will not object, but your auditor might, and it is far easier to answer before money starts moving than after.

One cosmetic consequence worth knowing: "UF Academy Foundation" is the developer name shown publicly beside the app on both stores. That reads well for a wellness programme, and less obviously so beside a paid subscription. Changing it later means re-verification with both stores, so it is worth a moment's thought now rather than a migration later.

02

Check the record before you start

Both stores verify against the Dun & Bradstreet record itself, not against what you type into their forms — and when the two disagree, the rejection rarely says which field was wrong. View the record free at iupdate.dnb.com, and correct it there if needed. Allow weeks for a correction to propagate, which is why this comes first rather than last.

  • Legal name, character for character. "Pvt Ltd" against "Private Limited" is enough to fail Google's verification.
  • A physical address. Google explicitly rejects a registered-agent address — it must be somewhere the business actually sits.
  • A phone number somebody answers. Apple calls it.

Confirm before anyone opens a form: exact registered name, registered address, and who holds authority to sign on the company's behalf. Both stores verify that person individually, and Google requires them to appear on the business registration.

03

Gather the paperwork once

Both stores want broadly the same evidence. Collecting it once, as clean PDFs in a shared folder, saves doing it twice under time pressure.

  • Certificate of Incorporation — or equivalent government registration
  • Proof of physical address — a utility bill or bank statement in the company's name. Google explicitly rejects a registered-agent address, so it must be somewhere the business actually sits
  • Authorised representative's photo ID — and they must be named on the business registration
  • A working phone number on the D‑U‑N‑S record — see the Apple track; this one derails more enrolments than anything else
  • A shared company email — apps@ufaslive.com or similar. Never a personal Gmail or a personal Apple ID; recovering an account tied to someone who has left is genuinely awful
04

The two tracks

Run these in parallel — they share no dependency beyond the entity and the D‑U‑N‑S number.

Google Play

$25 ONE-TIME · 2–5 DAYS TYPICAL

  1. Sign in to Play Console with the shared company Google account and choose Organisation, not Personal. This choice cannot be switched later.

  2. Pay the $25 registration fee. One time, never renewed.

  3. Enter the organisation details exactly as they appear on the D‑U‑N‑S record. A mismatched suffix — "Pvt Ltd" against "Private Limited" — is enough to fail verification.

  4. Upload the incorporation certificate, proof of physical address, and the authorised representative's ID.

  5. Wait for identity verification. Usually a few days; longer if anything mismatches.

  6. Create the payments profile. Needed before you can sell a subscription, and worth doing now rather than at launch.

Apple Developer

$99 / YEAR · 1–2 WEEKS TYPICAL

  1. Create a company Apple ID on the shared address and turn on two-factor authentication. Apple will not enrol an account without it.

  2. Start enrolment at developer.apple.com and choose Organisation.

  3. Give the legal entity name and D‑U‑N‑S number. Apple checks them against the Dun & Bradstreet record directly.

  4. Apple telephones the number on that record to verify you. Make sure someone answers it and knows the call is coming — an unanswered call is the single most common reason enrolment stalls for weeks.

  5. Pay the $99 annual fee once approved.

  6. In App Store Connect, accept the agreements and complete tax and banking details.

05

Two things worth knowing now

Good news — the testing gate does not apply to you

Google requires new personal accounts to run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 consecutive days before they may publish. Organisation accounts registered to a legal entity are exempt. Enrolling as a company therefore removes a fortnight of waiting from the launch, which is a second reason not to be tempted by a personal account to move faster.

Check this before any more payment work

Both stores generally require their own billing systems — Google Play Billing and Apple In-App Purchase — for digital subscriptions bought inside an app, and both take a commission. The plan to use Razorpay for the ₹399/month Plus subscription may not be permitted for in-app purchases, even though it is perfectly fine for payments taken on a website.

India's rules here have been contested and have shifted more than once, so this needs verifying against current policy rather than assumed either way. Worth resolving before anyone builds the payment flow, because the answer changes what gets built.
