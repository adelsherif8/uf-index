Who coaches whom

People sign up. Somebody has to decide which coach each one gets. There are three sensible ways to do it, and they give very different answers.

The situation

People do not join UF Index one at a time. They arrive in groups — a whole police station, a whole school. Six groups have signed up so far, a hundred people between them, and there are five coaches.

The three ways

To keep it simple, each example below shows one group of 12 people and three coaches. Same twelve people every time — only the rule changes.

WAY 1

Whoever has fewest clients

Each new person goes to whichever coach currently has the smallest caseload. Numbers stay even. Nobody is ever overloaded.

12 people from one police station

All twelve are split three ways. Colleagues who sit in the same room now have three different coaches, and get three different answers.

WAY 2

Take it in turns

First person to the first coach, second to the second, and round again. A strict rotation. Nobody can claim they were treated unfairly.

the same 12 people

The same problem, arrived at more predictably. It also ignores how hard each caseload is — ten quiet clients count the same as ten in crisis.

WAY 3

Keep each group together

Everyone from one organisation goes to the same coach, as long as that coach has room. That coach learns how a police shift rota works once.

the same 12 people

One coach, one group. She learns the shift pattern once, and everyone in that station gets the same answer. Caseloads end up uneven.

Now try it with the real numbers

All 100 people, all six groups, five coaches. Switch between the three ways and watch the bars change. Each coloured block is one group. A bar of one colour means one coach understands that whole group. A bar of five colours means nobody does.

—

Worth trying: drag the cap below 34 and watch Gurgaon Police break in two. That is the overflow problem — a group bigger than one caseload has to split, whichever rule you pick.

What I would pick

Way 3 — keep each group together, falling back to Way 1 when there is no room or no history to go on.

Even caseloads look tidier on a screen. But a coach who already understands how a police shift rota wrecks sleep is worth more than a coach with exactly twenty clients. And people in the same group talk to each other — "who's your coach" having one answer is easier for everyone.

Five things only you can decide

The rule above can be changed later. These cannot, once people are already with coaches.

Can a client ask for a particular coach? answer first

Not built. This matters more than the rule does — some clients will want a female coach, or someone who speaks their language. Asked after they have been assigned, it becomes a transfer, and that costs them the relationship they had started.

Decide this before automatic assignment is switched on. If clients can ask, the rule becomes a starting point rather than the answer — and that is a different thing to build.
How many clients is too many? the cap

It is set to 25, which is a number I made up. It is a hard limit — a full coach is skipped, and if every coach is full the system refuses rather than quietly overloading somebody.

Ask Parina and Manjula where coaching turns into admin. Their answer is the number.
When does someone get a coach? timing
  • The moment they sign up — everyone has a coach straight away, including people who never take a single check-in.
  • After their first check-in — only people who actually started.
  • When you decide — you work through the queue yourself.
After their first check-in. A caseload should be people, not sign-ups.
What if a group is bigger than one coach? overflow

Thirty-four Gurgaon Police clients do not fit in a caseload of 25.

  • Split it — 25 to one coach, 9 to another.
  • Move the whole group to someone with room.
  • Let that coach go over — a deliberate exception.
Split it, but always to the same second coach, so one station never has more than two.
If it goes lopsided, does it fix itself? drift

Say Parina ends up with 30 and Manjula with 5. Should the system move people across?

No. Moving a client halfway through costs them their coach and gains you a tidier number. Show it on the Coaches screen and let a person decide.
